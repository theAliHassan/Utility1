How Compound Interest Works

Compound interest is often called "interest on interest." When you earn interest, that interest gets added to your balance — and next time, you earn interest on the larger balance. Over time, this snowball effect can turn modest, consistent savings into a surprisingly large sum.

Simple vs. Compound Interest

With simple interest, you only ever earn interest on your original deposit. Put in $10,000 at 7% and you earn $700 every year — the same $700, forever.

With compound interest, that $700 gets added to your balance, so in year two you earn 7% on $10,700 — that's $749. In year three it's 7% on $11,449, and so on. The longer the time horizon, the more dramatic the difference.

A Worked Example

Suppose you invest $5,000 today, add $200 a month, and earn a 7% annual return, compounded monthly.

  • After 10 years: your balance is roughly $45,000. You contributed about $29,000 — the remaining $16,000 is pure interest.
  • After 20 years: your balance is roughly $124,000. You contributed about $53,000 — interest accounts for more than half.
  • After 30 years: your balance is roughly $285,000. You contributed about $77,000 — interest is now nearly three-quarters of the total.

Notice how the interest portion keeps growing even though your monthly contribution never changed. That's compounding doing its work.

Why Compounding Frequency Matters

"Compounded monthly" means your bank calculates interest twelve times a year and adds it to your balance each time, so you start earning interest on your interest sooner. "Compounded annually" does this only once a year. The difference is small in the short run, but over decades, monthly compounding can add thousands of dollars compared to annual compounding at the same stated rate.

The Key Takeaway

Time is the most powerful ingredient. Starting five years earlier — even with a smaller deposit — often beats starting later with more money. This is why financial advisors consistently say: the best time to start saving was yesterday; the second best time is today.
